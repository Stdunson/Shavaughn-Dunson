Why I'm Starting a Tech Blog

November 9, 2026

It's not often you see a student with a tech blog. And it makes sense. As a student, I often have the thought that nobody's really worried about what I have to say. It's not like I'm an expert at this stuff. I'm still learning. But that's the beauty of tech. One thing I've realized about this industry is that everybody is learning, regardless of their seniority. Thus, the things you have to say could serve useful, interesting, inspiring, or thought provoking to someone else.

A bit about me: I'm Shavaughn Dunson. As of my writing this, I'm a junior computer science student at the illustrious Prairie View A&M University. Building interesting and impactful software is something I've developed passion for over the years. My journey started as early as elementary school. They had us doing the hour of code lessons, the ones where you need to get a bee out of a maze. I didn't think much of it at the time, but little did I know the seed that was planted.

Fast forward to quarantine. When the COVID quarantine started, I was a bored 8th grader. They took the basketball rims in my neighborhood down, and with everyone inside, I needed to find a better way to spend my time than binge watching anime all day. That's when I discovered the Unity Game Engine. I'd interacted with scratch in the past, but Unity felt like unlocking the world. I felt I had the freedom to make whatever I wanted. For the next 1.5 years, game development was likely my greatest hobby. I made many little demo games, and the pool of unfinished projects was endless. I even participated in a game jam.

I'll never forget the feedback I got from that game jam. The theme was "out of control" and I decided to make a simple game where you play as a martian and dodge the asteroids. The twist was that the screen flipped intermittently, along with the controls. The gimmick was that the gravity was out of control. A fellow participant played my game, and to summarize his feedback, he said "You tried to say the gravity was out of control, but really all you did was flip the camera. Nice try though." At the time, I was a little salty, I though he could've let that one slide. But it taught me something about communication and overpromising. From there, I learned to take feedback with grace and an open mind. If you want to play the game, it's still online, and I'll link it below. All of the art, music, and UI was made by me in that game jam period.

Try my Game Jam Game!

After my game development days, I decided to take the computer science classes at my high school. Those were AP Computer Science Principles, AP Computer Science A, and an AI and Data Science intro class that they added during my senior year. The trend that I learned from those classes were twofold: the more I learn about Computer Science, the more my interest grows, and the part of CS I enjoy the most is the projects.

During college, I have been blessed with many great learning opportunities. I've held multiple internship and research experiences, participated in 4 hackathons(and even won one), published an iOS app, participated in the Codepath Tech Exchange(formerly Google Tech Exchange), attended 5 technical conferences, and met so many amazing people along the way. My interest in Computer Science has not waned, it has only grown.

One thing that I have made sure to do during my time at PV is involve myself in some of the CS orgs. At the time or writing, I'm currently the vice president of our school's Data Science Club, and I'm also involved in our ACM chapter, and I'm a participating member of other organizations. I like to use these as a place of discussion. A place to bounce ideas, teach and be taught, and expand my horizons. It's the same reason I like conferences so much. But there's issues with these. At conferences, the peer-peer conversations are often so brief that you don't end up getting deep into the topic at hand. And with orgs, it's hard to get more than a few meetings per month with actual attendees.

I feel like there's so many things I try, learn, or think about that I want to share with others. That's why I started this blog. Not only will it serve as a place for me to look back on, but a place to share any ideas or dilemmas I have which may expand someone's horizons. Also in doing this, I will be able to learn more from people's opinions of my writing. I plan to add a comment section to the blog so readers can share their thoughts and engage with my posts eventually, but for now feel free to shoot me an email or a message on LinkedIn.

The Main Idea

I decided to start a tech blog. I wanted a space to share my thoughts and the things I'm learning. This blog will be a place where I can document my journey and connect with others who share similar interests.
